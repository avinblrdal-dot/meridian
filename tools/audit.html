02During: the questions

Ask about what has happened, not what they would do. When something stands out, write their exact words in quotes.

Open with: Thanks for making time. This is free and there's nothing to buy today. I'll ask how your week actually runs, write down a few numbers, and at the end tell you honestly whether anything is worth fixing. If nothing is, I'll say so. Is it okay if I take notes?

Follow-up message

Plain text, for an email or a text. Fill in the brackets with what they actually told you. Keep it short and specific.

Hi [first name], Thanks again for making time for the Audit today. Here's a short summary, as promised. What I noticed: [one or two specific things, in their numbers, like how many calls a week go to voicemail while they're with a client]. What I'd fix first: [the one fix], because [one sentence, in their words]. What it would take from you: [for example, a short sit-down to go over your prices and policies]. No pressure either way. If you'd like to go ahead, just reply and I'll send a short form so it's set up around your hours, prices and policies. If now isn't a good time, that's completely fine, and I'm happy to answer any questions. Thanks, Avin Meridian, Frisco TX [your phone or email]

If nothing was worth fixing, send a shorter note: thank them, tell them the one thing they already do well, and say you're around if anything changes.
