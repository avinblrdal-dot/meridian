Before you go

Timing matters more than wording. A busy owner can't talk to you even if they want to.

When to walk in

  • Tuesday to Thursday, 10am to 2pm is the quiet stretch at most salons.
  • Skip Mondays (many salons are closed), Friday afternoons and Saturdays.
  • If every chair is full, don't start. Leave a card and come back.
  • Pick 3 to 5 businesses in one shopping center so one trip covers several.

What to bring

  • About 10 printed cards (section below).
  • Your phone with /diagnostic and /demo open in two tabs.
  • The visit log open, so you can fill it in from the car right after.
  • Nothing to sell. Today is for learning, not closing.

The 60-second script

Say it in your own words. The order matters more than the exact lines.

Open10 sec

Hi! Is the owner or manager around?

If they're with a client, ask when's a quieter time and come back then. That alone shows respect.

Who you are15 sec

Hi, I'm Avin. I'm a student here in Frisco and I'm starting a small business that helps salons with the phone and booking side of things. I'm not selling anything today. I'm trying to learn what actually eats up owners' time. Could I ask you three quick questions? Two minutes, tops.

Being upfront about being a student usually makes owners more willing to help, not less. "Not selling anything today" has to be true, so don't pitch in this visit.

Ask60 sec

1. What's the most annoying part of running the front desk week to week?

2. Think about the last time you missed a call because you were with a client. What happened? Did they call back?

3. What do you use for booking and reminders right now? Roughly what does it cost you?

Then stop talking and listen. Short answers are fine. Don't explain Meridian unless they ask.

Close15 sec

That's really helpful, thank you. I built a two-minute quiz that shows which fix would save a business like yours the most time. Here's a card if you want to try it later. And if you'd ever like a free Audit, where I look at how your bookings work and tell you honestly whether anything's worth fixing, the card has that too.

If they're curious right now, hand them your phone with /diagnostic open, or show /demo if missed calls came up.

If it's a no

No problem at all, thanks for your time. Can I leave a card?

Then leave. A polite no still goes in the log. How many places you visited, and how many said yes, is research data too.

If they have 5 more minutes

Ask about what has happened, not what they would do. "Would you use this?" gets a polite yes that means nothing. "What did you do last time?" gets the truth.

  1. How do most clients book with you: phone, an app, Instagram, walk-ins? Tells you which service even applies.
  2. Roughly how many calls a week go unanswered? How do you know? If they've never counted, that's a finding too.
  3. How many no-shows or late cancellations in a normal week? What do you do about them now?
  4. What questions do you answer over and over, by phone or DM?
  5. Have you tried any tool or person to fix this before? What happened, and why did you stop? Past spending is the strongest sign someone will pay again.
  6. What do you pay each month for your booking software and anything else like it? This is your price anchor. Write the actual number down.
  7. If one of these problems just disappeared, which one would you pick, and what would that be worth to you a month? Write their exact words and number. That's your willingness-to-pay evidence.
Never argue with an answer, correct them, or pitch halfway through. If they ask what you'd charge, say: I'm still working that out with businesses like yours. What would feel fair? Then write down what they say.

After every visit (do it in the car)

Five minutes, right away, before you forget. This log is your Capstone evidence: outreach attempts, customer needs and willingness to pay.

Write down

  • Business, type, date, who you talked to
  • Outcome: talked / left a card / come back later / not interested
  • Their biggest pain, in their exact words
  • What they use and pay now
  • Anything they said about price or value
  • Next step and date
